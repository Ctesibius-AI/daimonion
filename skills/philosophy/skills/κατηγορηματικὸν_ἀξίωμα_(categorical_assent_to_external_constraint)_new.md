# Κατηγορηματικὸν ἀξίωμα (Categorical Assent to External Constraint) — The Stoics distinguished between ἐφ' ἡμῖν (what is up to us — internal) and οὐκ ἐφ' ἡμῖν (what is not — external). For governance of behavior, external constraints that cannot be bypassed are structurally more reliable than internal commitments, because they do not depend on the governing agent being in an uncorrupted state.

*Discovered: 2026-04-13*
*Source: Synapsis analysis*

## WHY
'The guard that matters most is the one you can't skip.' QA Auditor chosen as visibility tool, not auto-repair. Stop hook as external behavioral enforcer rather than internal mirror.

## HOW
Chatzi's preference for external, unskippable constraints over internal self-governance operates on the same structural logic: when you cannot trust the governed agent to govern itself reliably (whether because it is young, because it overclaims, or because self-interest corrupts), move the enforcement outside. The principle is not distrust — it is correct epistemic placement of the verification function.

## Philosophical Lineage
Stoic philosophy placed the locus of reliable action in external practice (discipline, habit, community accountability) precisely because pure internal intention was insufficient. Marcus Aurelius's Meditations are external writing-to-self because internal intention alone doesn't hold. Same structure.
