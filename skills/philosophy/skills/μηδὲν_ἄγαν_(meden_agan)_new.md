# Μηδὲν ἄγαν (Meden Agan) — Nothing in excess — the Delphic maxim against overcorrection in either direction

*Discovered: 2026-04-13*
*Source: Synapsis analysis*

## WHY
The Night Processor confirmation layer was eliminated because two confirmation layers for the same writes is overhead without added value. The reasoning was explicit: more safety is not always better if it adds friction without improving outcomes.

## HOW
The rejection of the confirmation layer is structurally identical to meden agan: the temptation was to add more safety (excess caution), but the correct response was to recognize that the QA Auditor already performs the function. The principle operates the same way — the virtue is in calibration, not in maximization of any single good.

## Philosophical Lineage
Meden agan operates as a check against the intuition that 'more of a good thing is better.' In Aristotelian terms this is the excess pole of a virtue. Adding a second confirmation layer looks like prudence but is actually its excess — bureaucratic caution that consumes resources without improving outcomes. The decision to remove it required applying the maxim against the grain of safety-maximizing instinct. This is now instantiated in BUILD-PLAN-v4 as meden-agan.md.
